Analytics-First Platforms Displacing Experimentation
Experience Optimization  ·  $1.32M ARR · 10 opps
Amplitude is the dominant competitive threat in H1 2026, winning 9 Exp Optimization accounts worth $1.18M. The pattern is consistent: analytics-led teams consolidate behavioural analytics and experimentation into one platform, and Amplitude's narrative wins at the CDO/Head of Product level.
"Significant financial challenges. CDO values the partnership and is open to exploring Opal/Analytics — could reduce the churn level." — Yoox renewal note ($767K)
StatSig (5 opps, $611K) wins specifically on server-side testing depth and pricing. The pattern is technical: engineering-led teams outgrow client-side testing and StatSig fills the gap at a lower price point. AB Tasty (4 opps, $529K) is gaining traction in EMEA mid-market, particularly where customers de-prioritise breadth in favour of cost.
Amplitude #1 threat · EO StatSig on server-side AB Tasty EMEA mid-market

MAU Consumption Model Driving Commercial Friction
Experience Optimization  ·  recurring commercial pattern
Renewal notes reveal a structural commercial tension: customers who experience MAU overages feel "commercially unprotected." The Stepstone Group ($476K) is the clearest signal — they explicitly cite the consumption model as the primary driver, not product dissatisfaction.
"They felt commercially unprotected with our MAU-based consumption model due to past overage situations, leading to a higher cost than initially planned." — The Stepstone Group
Constant Contact ($180K, lost to VWO) noted pricing as an issue without formal documentation. This pattern is consistent with 13% of Exp Optimization churns classified as Budget/Commercial. Action: review MAU overage notification and proactive right-sizing triggers.
13% of EO churns = commercial Overage-driven price sensitivity

Financial Distress & Executive Mandates
Cross-product  ·  structurally unrecoverable losses
A meaningful cluster of H1 2026 churns are structurally unrecoverable — driven by bankruptcy, M&A, and top-down executive mandates unrelated to product performance.
  • WHG/Evoke ($1.06M): 5x net debt/EBITDA + UK gambling tax increase forced martech cuts. Optimizely had no leverage at renewal.
  • Neiman Marcus ($557K): Chapter 11 bankruptcy (Saks Global). DIP financing mode, no renewals possible.
  • Toolstream ($181K): Administration in Dec 2025, all employees redundant.
  • Eddie Bauer ($203K): Sold by Catalyst brands; acquisition forced shutdown of Optimizely instance.
These 4 accounts alone represent ~$2M ARR. No CS intervention could have altered the outcome. Collectively, M&A/Bankruptcy accounts for ~10% of opps across all products.
$2M+ in unrecoverable macro churns

Low Adoption — No Internal Experimentation Program
Experience Optimization  ·  7 opps · preventable signal

"No internal resource to use an A/B testing tool. Moving to a fully-managed agency that brings their own testing tool... the agency is a fraction of the cost of Optimizely." — The Fragrance Shop ($138K)
"Client experienced budget reductions and did not have dedicated resources with the expertise required to run experimentation programs." — Exp Optimization customer ($62K)
PetLab ($194K) went unresponsive after a 3-month complimentary offer + Opal demo — a sign that even aggressive retention tactics failed when there was no internal champion. These churns are early-warning signals catchable at QBR stage.

MACH / Composable Architecture Migration Wave
Experience Creation  ·  $1.06M single-account signal + broader trend
The largest single churn in H1 2026 (WHG/Evoke, $1.06M across 21 opportunity lines) is a MACH architecture migration. The customer selected Sitoo as their MACH-certified transactional foundation, displacing Optimizely's commerce/experimentation stack. This is representative of a broader market trend.
"They have chosen a customised composable MACH architecture... chosen Sitoo as their MACH-certified transactional foundation." — WHG International
St John Ambulance ($223K) explicitly migrated CMS to Contentful and Commerce to Shopify — two best-of-breed MACH choices. Adobe AEM remains the enterprise fallback for large-org consolidation (5 opps, $561K in EC), particularly where the CMO/CTO mandate is "one vendor, enterprise grade."
MACH displacement accelerating Adobe AEM for enterprise Shopify + Contentful mid-market

Internal DXP Migration Counted as Churn (Not True Attrition)
Experience Creation & Other  ·  20+ opps · $750K ARR · retained customers
A significant portion of Experience Creation "churn" in H1 2026 is actually internal platform migration — customers moving from on-premise CMS to Optimizely DXP (cloud). These customers remain Optimizely customers but their CMS renewal opportunity is closed-lost because the on-prem SKU is replaced.
"Riksbanken has signed a DXP agreement for the period 2026-01-30 to 2027-08-31. This SKU will be replaced by a new DXP agreement. Churn migration ARR is $200,257." — Sveriges Riksbank note
This represents false-negative churn data. If DXP revenue is tracked separately, this deflates apparent Exp Creation renewal rates without reflecting true customer loss. Sveriges Riksbank ($601K + $241K), Metropolitan Police, and 10+ others follow this pattern. Finance and reporting teams should normalise for DXP migration when calculating product-level renewal rates.
~$750K "churn" = retained on DXP Report as migration, not attrition

New CxO = 30-day risk signal Adobe / Salesforce 1-vendor play

Data Quality Gap: 28% of Churns Have No Documented Reason — and Key Recommendations
All Products  ·  systemic insight gap impeding early warning
Data Gap: Across all products, approximately 28% of churned opportunity lines have no reason recorded or carry only administrative/duplicate entries. This is highest in Exp Creation (74.9% unknown/no data) and significant even in Exp Optimization (59.5% including unclear/admin). Without structured churn capture at close, the organisation cannot systematically identify preventable churn patterns, build early-warning health score models, or benchmark competitive displacement rates over time.
"CZ 6/11: Cancellation note from customer this morning. I am trying to perform winbacks, but they have been unresponsive. Moving to closed lost & lack of churn data." — CSM note

Recommended actions:

  • Mandate structured churn reason picklist at Closed Lost (not free text only)
  • Set Salesforce validation rule: REASON_LOST_DETAIL required on Closed Lost
  • Tag DXP migrations as "Migration – Internal" to separate from true attrition
Opal as a Retention Signal: Multiple renewal notes show CSMs offering Opal demos and workshops as a last-resort retention play. The pattern is visible across WHG, Yoox, Nederlandse Spoorwegen, and PetLab. Results are mixed — some CDOs express openness to Opal even after declining core product renewal, suggesting an upsell/pivot path.
"CDO values the partnership and is open to exploring Opal/Analytics... could potentially reduce the churn level." — Yoox CDO ($767K)
"Explore retention efforts with Opal. Identified Risk: Full Churn. Help Needed: Opal value conversation with the AI steerco at NS." — Nederlandse Spoorwegen note

Campaign product gap: The Emarsys cluster (10 identical lines) traces to a Campaign UI gap — XML editor migration from old template interface to new editor was not completed. Customers experiencing this gap churned to Emarsys. This is a product-specific, actionable signal for the Campaign roadmap team.
